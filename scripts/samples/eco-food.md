# Food chains and food webs

**Field:** ecology  
**Module:** Ecosystem Science  
**Concept id:** eco-food

## 1. What a food chain is

A **food chain** is a single pathway that shows who eats whom. Energy moves from one organism to the next when one is eaten.

Simple example:

**grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk**

- **Grass** is a **producer** (it makes food by photosynthesis).
- **Grasshopper** is a **primary consumer** (herbivore).
- **Frog** is a **secondary consumer**.
- **Snake** is a **tertiary consumer**.
- **Hawk** is a top (apex) consumer in this chain.

Real ecosystems almost never run on one chain alone. They form **food webs**: many chains linked because most animals eat more than one food and are eaten by more than one predator.

## 2. Producers, consumers, decomposers

| Role | What they do | Examples |
|------|----------------|----------|
| **Producers (autotrophs)** | Capture energy, usually from sunlight, and build organic matter | Plants, algae, cyanobacteria |
| **Consumers (heterotrophs)** | Get energy by eating other organisms | Herbivores, carnivores, omnivores |
| **Decomposers** | Break down dead material and waste | Bacteria, fungi |

Decomposers are essential. Without them, nutrients stay locked in dead matter.

## 3. Trophic levels

1. **Level 1** — producers  
2. **Level 2** — primary consumers  
3. **Level 3** — secondary consumers  
4. **Level 4** — tertiary consumers  

Omnivores can occupy more than one level.

## 4. The 10% rule and energy loss

Only about **10%** of usable energy typically becomes biomass at the next trophic level. The rest is lost as heat, incomplete digestion, and metabolism. That is why food chains are short and apex predators are rare. **Energy pyramids** are always upright.

## 5. Food webs vs food chains

- **Chain:** one teaching path.  
- **Web:** many linked paths (closer to nature).  

Removing a species can cause a **trophic cascade** (e.g. fewer otters → more urchins → less kelp).

## 6. Grazing vs detrital pathways

Much energy can flow through **dead organic matter** into detritivores and microbes, not only through living plants to herbivores.

## 7. How ecologists study this

Stomach analysis, stable isotopes, predator exclusion experiments, and energy-flow models.

## 8. Human impacts

Overfishing, habitat loss, invasives, and climate change all rewire webs.

## 9. Common mistakes

- Forgetting producers  
- Arrows the wrong way (arrows show energy **to the eater**)  
- Saying energy is recycled like nutrients (energy **flows**; nutrients **cycle**)

## 11. Revision points

- Producers form the base.  
- Webs are more realistic than single chains.  
- ~10% transfer explains short chains.  
- Decomposers recycle nutrients; energy is still lost as heat.
