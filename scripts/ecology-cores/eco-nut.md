# Nutrient cycles

**Field:** ecology · **Module:** Ecosystem Science · **Concept id:** eco-nut

---

Organisms are built from chemical elements that must be taken up, retained and returned. Nutrient cycles describe movements between living tissues and abiotic reservoirs. Unlike energy, nutrients can be reused if decomposers, weathering and transport keep them available.

## Pools, fluxes and limitation

A pool is a quantity of an element in a compartment; a flux is a rate of movement between pools. Growth is often limited by the nutrient in shortest supply relative to demand—commonly nitrogen or phosphorus on land and in fresh water.

## Carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus

Carbon moves among atmosphere, organisms, soils, oceans and rocks. Photosynthesis and respiration dominate the fast cycle; fossil carbon release links ecology to climate. Nitrogen gas is abundant but mostly unavailable until fixed by microbes or industry; excess reactive nitrogen causes eutrophication. Phosphorus lacks a large gas pool, weathers from rock, and often limits freshwater production.

## Decomposers

Without decomposition, nutrients stay locked in dead matter. Microbial and detritivore activity return elements to forms producers can use. Temperature, moisture and litter chemistry control rates.
